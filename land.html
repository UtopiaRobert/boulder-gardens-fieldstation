Patterns of Human Use

Permaculture zones at Boulder Gardens

At Boulder Gardens, zones describe patterns of habitation, infrastructure, movement, and human attention. They are not rings around a single garden or residence.

Because the sanctuary has several dispersed living areas, its zones appear as separate islands of activity within a much larger wild landscape. The maps document how the land is currently used and can change as those patterns evolve.

Zone 1

Frequent activity

The most-used shared areas and places with concentrated infrastructure, visited and observed throughout the day.

Zone 2

Private habitation

Trailers, tiny homes, yurts, and the immediate surroundings of these private living spaces.

Zone 3

Camping and circulation

Campsites and places people regularly walk through, gather in, or use less intensively.

Zone 5

Untouched sanctuary

Most of Boulder Gardens remains unmarked and completely untouched—a place for habitat, observation, and natural processes.

Land & Ecology · Community observation

The Four Corners Hike

Walking the boundary of Boulder Gardens

On February 23, 2026, members of the Boulder Gardens community spent the day walking the perimeter of the sanctuary. The route crossed ridges, washes, boulder fields, roads, and open desert around the 640-acre property.

The walk took about eight hours. The distance was roughly six miles—an estimate rather than a recorded GPS track—and the constant climbing and descending made the scale of the land much more tangible than a map alone can.

It was both an observation day and a shared experience: a way to learn the sanctuary by moving through its edges together.
